# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What the game is

A single-player hex-and-counter wargame in the browser. Each scenario sets up the factions and says whether each one is human- or computer-controlled.

- **Combat is HeroQuest-style dice**: the attacker rolls its attack dice and the target rolls its defence dice. Each die scores with a chance set by its colour: white 1/3, red 1/2, black 2/3. Each block cancels one hit, every hit left costs one body point, and a unit at 0 body points dies.
- **Weapons hold the attack**: each `Weapon` in `WEAPONS` has attack dice and a min/max range in hexes. The dagger is 1 white die at range 1; the sling is 1 white die at range 2–3. **Units** have defence dice, body points, movement points and a `Weapons` bit_set. The weapon used is chosen automatically by distance (`weapon_for`, strongest dice if several reach), so a slinger 'switches' to its dagger in melee at no cost. When the browser pane is hidden it draws no frames, so clicks and keys seem to do nothing in screenshots: step frames by hand with `memory.exports.step(0.016, memory.exports.default_context_ptr())`. Unit types (each drawn with its own Anvil sprite): Kobold (dagger), Kobold Slinger (sling + dagger), Heavy Kobold (axe 1 red; chain; body 2; move 3), Kobold Champion (sword 1 white + 1 red; plate; body 3), Kobold Scout (dagger; move 6, sight 8), Kobold Archer (bow 1 white, range 2-5, + dagger; sight 7), Kobold Crossbow (crossbow 1 white, range 2-4, **pierces**, + dagger; leather; move 3). Kobold Pike (pike 1 red, range 1, **strikes first**; leather; move 3): `move_unit` walks the path and, after each step, every enemy pike next to the new hex that has not `reacted` this turn strikes the mover through `resolve_attack(..., first_strike = true)` (shared with `attack`, but costing the pike nothing); a kill ends the move there, otherwise it walks on; the move is pushed as Move events split around each strike (`push_move_event`), and `reacted` clears whenever any turn starts. The tactical AI dislikes stepping next to a ready pike it can see (`PIKE_DREAD`, a terrain-bonus penalty). Kobold Wololo (no weapon, `Unit_Type.chants`): `chant(g, caster, target)` (checked by `can_chant`: in sight, within `CHANT_RANGE` 3, line of sight beyond 1) blesses a friend or curses an enemy (`Unit.boon`, `Boon`), ending the Wololo's move like an attack; `resolve_attack` spends both units' boons on their roll (blessed = advantage, cursed = **disadvantage**: `roll_dice(..., disadvantage = true)` re-rolls each scoring die and keeps it only if it scores again; with both, neither), and unused boons fade when the caster's side's turn starts (`boon_by`). `unit_finished` also counts a chant still to make. The AI plays Wololos first in its turn (`ai_chanter_unit`, `ai_chant_value`: bless a friend that can strike now, else one in reach of the enemy; curse an enemy our units can reach; weighted by points); tactical attackers prefer cursed targets. On the page, a selected Wololo curses an enemy on click; blessing a friend needs chant mode (C or the Chant button) because a click on a friend selects it. Badges: gold + blessed, purple - cursed. A piercing weapon (`Weapon.pierce`) makes the target roll `defence_against(ut, w)`: its best die (black, red, white) removed, one die always kept. **Armour** (`Armour_Kind`, `ARMOURS`, `Unit_Type.armour`) adds defence dice to the hide in `Unit_Type.defense`: leather +1 white, chain +1 red, plate +1 black; always roll `unit_defence(ut)`. Damage stays (no healing); units with more than 1 body point show pips on the counter and the log says "wounded". The fog view cache is per sight range (`VIEW_SLOTS` 4), not per unit kind, so `Game` stays small enough for test stacks. The 4-unit teams are dagger, slinger, slinger, dagger; the 20-vs-20 battle is 15 kobolds and 5 slingers a side. **Points** (`Unit_Type.points`, `army_points`): kobold 10, slinger 12, heavy 25, champion 55, scout 11, archer 14, crossbow 16, pike 20, wololo 12, set with the simulator so equal points fight about even; `tools/sim.sh` prints each army's points. Each unit can move (spending points over several moves) and then attack once per turn. **Attacking ends the unit's move** (`attack` zeroes `moves_left` and sets `done`). A unit can also finish its move by choice (`finish_move`; the UI says "Finish move", never "end unit", which reads like destroying it). **A side's turn cannot end until every unit's move is over**: `end_turn` returns false while `units_left_to_act > 0`. A unit's move is over when `unit_finished` is true: it was finished, it attacked, or it has no movement left and no enemy in reach. `unit_finished` / `unit_has_moved` drive the map shading: a finished unit is greyed out, and a unit that has moved but can still act gets a white pip.
- **Terrain sets the movement cost**: grass 1, forest 2, road 1. A step from road to road costs only 1/2; leaving a road costs the full cost of the hex entered. Movement is therefore counted in **half points** internally (`HALVES_PER_POINT`): `moves_left` and `movement_costs` are in halves, while `Unit_Type.move` and `TERRAIN_MOVE_COST` are in whole points. Units can pass through friendly units but not enemies, and cannot stop on an occupied hex.
- **Map**: 20×20 staggered ("odd-r") grid of pointy-topped hexes, where odd rows are shifted half a hex to the right.
- **Fortify** (`fortify`, key D): only before the unit has moved or attacked, and it is the unit's whole turn. Until its side's next turn starts, the unit defends with **advantage**: each failed defence die is rolled once more (`roll_dice(..., advantage = true)`; white 1/3 → 5/9). `start_faction_turn` clears it. The AI fortifies only when it cannot attack this turn but some enemy could attack it next turn (`ai_threatened`, an exact check with that enemy's real movement costs; a distance estimate could leave both sides fortified forever). Fortified units have a pale ring on the map and "(fort)" in the combat log.
- **Line of sight**: attacks beyond 1 hex need it (`line_of_sight` in `rules.odin`, `hexes_between` in `hex.odin`). Forest, or a living unit of another faction than the shooter's, on any hex strictly between the two units blocks it (`line_of_sight(g, a, b, viewer)`; `NO_FACTION` checks terrain only); the end hexes never block, so units can shoot into and out of a forest. A line running exactly along a hex edge is clear if either side is (lines are traced twice, nudged each way). On the staggered map, lines between edge hexes can graze off-map positions, which never block. The shooter's own side never blocks.
- **Elevation** (`Game.high`, `is_high`): each hex is low or high, independent of terrain. Climbing onto high ground costs +1 point, or +1/2 when both hexes are road (`step_cost(from, to, from_high, to_high)`, `CLIMB_HALVES`). In melee the higher unit has advantage, attacking or defending (`combat_advantage`; advantage never stacks with fortify). A ranged weapon fired from high onto low ground has max range x1.5 rounded down (`weapon_max_range`; sling 3 -> 4). In `line_of_sight`, high ground between two low ends blocks; if either end is high, low-ground forest and enemies no longer block but high-ground ones do. `grow_hills_to(percent)` makes the "some hills" (15%) and "hilly" (35%) skirmishes; older scenarios stay flat. Drawn as paler hexes with a dark rim. The combat log tags advantage as "(high)" or "(fort)".
- **Fog of war** (`fog.odin`, on in every scenario via `new_game`; `g.fog` false, as in `blank_game` and `tools/sim.sh -nofog`, means everyone sees everything). Vision per unit type (`Unit_Type.vision`: kobold 5, slinger 7) with `line_of_sight(..., NO_FACTION)`, so units never block sight; a unit in forest is only seen within `FOREST_SIGHT` (2). Per faction, `Hex_Mask` row bitmasks: `seen` (explored, permanent), `in_view` (now), `watched` (a unit here would be seen). Each hex's view per unit kind is cached in `g.view_cache` because terrain never changes during a game: call `clear_view_cache` if a test changes the map after units have looked. `update_vision(g, looking)` runs after moves, kills, `end_turn` and `new_game`; `last_seen` markers record where an enemy was last seen (also along a mover's path) and are cleared only when the `looking` side (the mover, or the side starting its turn) watches the hex and finds it empty. Rules under fog: `attack_weapon` needs the target visible; `movement_costs` blocks only enemies the viewer can see (default the unit's own side), and `move_unit` walks the `path_to` hex by hex, stopping in front of a hidden enemy (keeping unspent points, logging "blocks the way"). **The AI uses only what its side sees** (`ai_view`): visible enemies for attacks, those plus markers for approaching, and with neither it scouts: the unit with the longest vision heads for the unexplored hex nearest by way of the map centre while the rest follow `SCOUT_LEAD` hexes behind. The page shows the human's view; with no human, V cycles everything / Red / Blue.

**`DESIGN.md` holds the user's design decisions, including ones not built yet (computer player improvements, animation, art and sound, touch) and the agreed order of work.**

## Commands

```bash
tools/sim.sh 2k2s 4k -n 1000 -forest 50 -hills 15   # balance simulator: computer-vs-computer battles, win rates, per-weapon kill rates (no args = usage)
./build.sh                      # odin -> build/web/game.wasm (+ index.html, odin.js); OPT=size for shipping
./test.sh                       # native tests of src/game
./test.sh -define:ODIN_TEST_NAMES=game.test_dice_odds   # one test
python3 -m http.server -d build/web 8123                # serve (wasm needs http, not file://)
tools/pages.sh                  # size build copied into docs/ for GitHub Pages (does not commit or push)
tools/shots.sh                  # assets/screenshots/ and assets/cover.png via headless Chrome (+ tools/make_cover.py)
tools/ship.sh                   # itch.io zip; --push refuses until TARGET is set (no itch.io page yet). shippit.sh wraps it
```

Release layout follows the vault's `Tech/Release standard.md`: `ITCH_DESCRIPTION.md`, `assets/cover.png` (630x500), `assets/screenshots/`, `devlog/YYYYMMDD/devlog.md`. `?steps=N` (with `?scenario=N`) plays N computer actions at load; `tools/shots.sh` uses it because headless Chrome's virtual time does not run the AI timer.

**GitHub Pages** (live since November 5, 2026): https://playdeezgames.github.io/skirmishers-of-splorr/ is served from `docs/` on `main`. `docs/` is committed build output, so do not edit it by hand. To update the page, run `tools/pages.sh`, commit `docs/`, and push, but only when the user says push.

`.claude/launch.json` defines the `skirmishers` preview server on port 8123. `?scenario=N` (1-based index into `SCENARIOS`) loads a scenario straight away, skipping the title screen (the menus' Play list has them all): 2 is the 4-vs-4 and 3 the 20-vs-20 computer-vs-computer battle, both useful for watching the AI and combat. Both scripts run with `-vet -strict-style`.

## Architecture

- `src/game/`: package `game`, all of the rules, with **no browser imports**, so it builds and tests natively.
  - `hex.odin`: offset↔cube conversion, neighbours (different offsets for even and odd rows), distance, and hex↔pixel conversion.
  - `rules.odin`: terrain and dice tables, `UNIT_TYPES` (indexed by `Unit_Kind`), the `Game` struct (fixed-size arrays and a ring-buffer log, no allocation), `step_cost(from, to)`, Dijkstra `movement_costs` (fixed-size binary heap; a linear scan was ~10× slower and made the AI tests take 20 s), `move_unit`, `attack`, `end_turn`, and win checks. `move_unit` and `attack` refuse units that are not on the current faction's side.
  - `scenario.odin`: the `SCENARIOS` table (name + setup proc). `new_game(g, scenario, seed)` resets everything. Forest is grown in clumps from random seed hexes, then `generate_road` lays a road from one edge to the opposite edge (east–west or north–south, chosen at random) by stepping to a random neighbour closer to the far end. Forest under a deployment hex is cleared to grass. The forest variants (25/50/75%) instead lay the road, place the armies, then `grow_forest_to(percent)` grows clumps on the remaining grass until exactly that share of the 400 hexes is forest.
  - `custom.odin`: custom battles. `Custom_Battle` holds the forest level, road, hills, budget, who plays, and each side's army choice (an `ARMY_PRESETS` index, `ARMY_CUSTOM` with `Army_Counts` bought within the budget, or `ARMY_RANDOM` drawn by `random_army` from the game's rng); `new_custom_game` lays the road, places both armies with `place_block` (inset 2, `REAR_ORDER` puts shooters and the Wololo in the back column), then grows forest and hills. `new_game` and `new_custom_game` share `begin_game` / `finish_setup`; `g.name` is what the panel shows.
  - `fog.odin`: vision, explored hexes, last-seen markers and the view cache (see Fog of war above).
  - `ai.odin`: `ai_step` plays one computer unit per call (or ends the turn), so the page can show each action. `ai_take_turn` runs a whole turn, for tests. Two styles per faction (`Faction.ai`, `Ai_Style`): **Tactical** (the default, zero value, used by every scenario) and **Basic** (the first AI, kept for `tools/sim.sh -red basic` comparisons; `ai_unit`). Basic moves each unit to the best hex by `ai_hex_score` (ranged shot with no enemy adjacent, then any attack, then closest to a known enemy), then attacks the weakest target in reach. Tactical (`ai_tactical_unit`) scores hexes by `[ai_hex_score, -terrain bonus, cost]`: before contact it holds outside `ai_threat_map` (hexes any known enemy, at its real or last-seen hex, could attack next turn: its reachable hexes grown by its reach with `mask_grow`, line of sight ignored; cached per turn in `g.ai_threat` / `g.ai_reach`), giving up after `HOLD_TURNS` quiet turns (`g.ai_held`) and as soon as anyone attacked within a round (`g.contact_turn`); the bonus favours safe hexes, high ground and forest; the scout prefers forest (`SCOUT_COVER`); `ai_tactical_target` prefers targets that are not fortified or uphill, slingers first. It fortifies only when it cannot get out of reach.
  - `game_test.odin`: has `#+build !js`, which is required because Odin otherwise compiles `_test` files into the wasm build and fails on `core:os`.
- **Randomness belongs to the game** (`Rng`, splitmix64 in `g.rng`) and is seeded by `new_game`. Use explicit `u64` because `int` is 32 bits on `js_wasm32`. Tests pick seeds to force outcomes.
- `tools/sim/` imports the game package by relative path and plays `ai_step` battles. Armies are placed with `place_block` (rear units first). `Game.attacks` / `Game.kills` count attacks and kills per faction and weapon for it. Findings so far: the starting distance between the armies (`-inset`) decides who strikes first and is worth up to ±13 points of win rate; one extra unit is worth 60–76%; slingers beat plain kobolds (4s v 4k ≈ 59%) because a sling is free extra reach, In dense forest line of sight cancels most of that edge (4s v 4k: 65% before line of sight, 55% with it, at 75% forest). Tactical beats basic 53–55% in 4-unit skirmishes and is about even in 15k5s; making the first attack is worth little on its own (a dagger blow kills 2 in 9).
- **Events and animation:** the rules push an `Event` (Move with its path, Attack with its `Attack_Result`, Fortify) into the `g.events` ring (`push_event`, `EVENT_RING`); `src/anim.odin` (web package) plays them one at a time (`anim_update`, `anim_unit`, `draw_anim_effects`), showing under fog only what the viewer watches. `anim_busy()` holds back `ai_step` and the player's clicks and keys; `anim_skip()` drops the queue (new game, `?steps=N`). Sounds start with each event, the hit, block or slain sound when the blow lands.
- **Art and sound** are made in the Anvil (MCP server `anvil-prod`; world saved as `/home/yermom/anvil-worlds/skirmishers-of-splorr.json`, exported to `assets/art/world.json`): 16x16 sprites in set `art` (kobold, slinger, trees), palette index 0 transparent. `tools/gen_atlas.py` (stdlib only, run by `build.sh`) packs them into `build/web/art.png`; `Sprite` in `src/main.odin` must follow the sprite-set order, and `blit(sprite, cx, cy, scale)` draws them (units and trees at 2x). `assets/sfx/*.wav` come from `sound_export`; `Sound` in `src/main.odin` must match `SOUND_FILES` in `web/index.html`, which fetches and decodes them on the first key or pointer press. Mute (M or the Sound button) lives in the page (`muted`, saved in localStorage as `skirmishers.muted`); Odin reads it through the `is_muted` / `toggle_mute` imports. After editing the world in Anvil, re-export it over `assets/art/world.json`.
- `src/ui.odin` (`#+build js`): menus and input. Seven buttons (`Input`: up, down, left, right, OK, cancel, menu) from every device: keys in `on_key` (arrows/WASD, Space/Enter, Esc/Backspace, Tab/P; letters F C M E V as shortcuts), the gamepad polled in `web/index.html` (`pollPads`, standard mapping, held directions repeat) and sent through the `press` export, the mouse (pointing moves the cursor or menu highlight, a click is OK). Screens are a stack of `Menu_Id`s whose items `menu_items` rebuilds from the state each frame (`activate` runs an `Action`): Title (over a computer battle, `BACKDROP_SCENARIO`, that restarts when it ends), Scenarios, Options (auto end turn, sound, computer speed; saved with the `load_option`/`save_option` imports in localStorage), Help, Pause, Unit (fortify / finish / chant), Confirm (restart or quit mid-battle), Game_Over, Custom (the custom battle's settings; Left / Right step a setting the other way, `menu_dir`) and Army (buy units for `building_side`; Left sells, the mouse's - box too). The custom setup lives in `custom` and is saved to localStorage under option keys from `CUSTOM_KEY` / `CUSTOM_COUNTS_KEY`; `scenario == CUSTOM_SCENARIO` (-1) means restart builds from it. With no menu open, a hex cursor moves (up/down keep to the column you were heading in across the staggered rows), OK acts on the hex under it (`play_ok`, what a click does) and Cancel leaves chant mode, drops the selection, then opens Pause. The computer only plays while no menu is open (except the title's battle). **The turn ends by itself** (`auto_end`, the default) half a second after none of the player's units can act; the option makes the player press End turn instead.
- `src/main.odin` (`#+build js`): the web layer. It owns the game state for the page (selection, cursor, AI timer, current scenario), the map and the panel (unit card, the hex under the cursor, End turn only in that option, a Menu button, the log, a line saying what OK and Cancel do now). Finishing a unit selects the next one still to act and moves the cursor to it, and so does the start of the player's turn; the info for a hex says when something blocks the selected unit's shot. It calls `ai_step` every `AI_DELAY` seconds on computer turns, and draws everything each frame. Drawing goes through a few `foreign import canvas` procs in canvas pixels. Input arrives as `proc "c"` exports (`mouse_move`, `mouse_click`, `load_scenario`), plus a key listener.
- `web/index.html`: the 2D-canvas shim (hexes, circles, text via `memory.loadString`) and pointer → canvas-pixel mapping. If you add an import, add it to both the Odin `foreign` block and the `imports.canvas` object.
